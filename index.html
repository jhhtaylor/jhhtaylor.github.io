Distributed Systems Deep Dive

Each episode starts with the headline: a bank that forgot everyone's balance, a database that answered with nothing. Then it goes through how the system normally works, where the flaw was and how the failure spread. The last part is the bit most outage videos skip. I look at why the team built it that way (a deadline, a vendor contract, what the industry knew at the time), reproduce the failure in a small program, and show the fix I'd actually ship on a real team.

  1. 01

    The Night a Bank Forgot Everyone's Balance

    RBS/NatWest, June 2012

    A scheduler change broke the overnight batch on a mainframe core and stalled payments for millions. Introduces CAP and PACELC.

  2. 02

    Why a Central Bank Relocated $10 Billion in Gold

    De Nederlandsche Bank, 2026

    86 tonnes of gold moved across the Atlantic, partly by plane and partly on paper, treated as a zero-downtime migration.

  3. 03

    Phantom Nodes: The Cassandra Ring

    Monzo, July 2019

    Six nodes joined the ring without their data, so quorum reads came back "row not found". Consistent hashing, replication and quorum.

  4. 04

    The Race That Took Down Half the Internet

    AWS us-east-1, October 2025

    Two DNS enactors raced a check-then-act and left DynamoDB's endpoint empty. Reproduced with goroutines, fixed with compare-and-swap.

  5. 05

    Christmas Day, Paid Twice

    Santander UK, 2021 · coming next

    About 75,000 payments sent a second time on Christmas morning. Idempotency keys, and why "same payee, same amount" is the wrong check.

The code behind the episodes

distributed-systems-deep-dive has one folder per episode, each with a small runnable Go model of the mechanism and tests that pin down the behaviour shown in the video. Where a company's real code isn't public, the model is a reconstruction from the postmortem, and the episode's README says so. 

go test ./...                          # every episode's tests
go run ./ep03-monzo-quorum/cmd/demo    # one episode's demo
  • ep01-rbs-batch-vs-queue A batch run halting on one bad job vs an event-driven queue with a dead-letter queue
  • ep02-dnb-gold-migration A live migration over two independent paths where the total never changes
  • ep03-monzo-quorum A token ring where nodes join without data, and the quorum reads that follow
  • ep04-aws-dns-race Two enactors racing a check-then-act, fixed with compare-and-swap
  • ep05-santander-duplicate-payments A scheduler that pays twice, and an idempotency key claimed before the money moves
